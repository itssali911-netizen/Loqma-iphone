## Automatic Health implementation

Observers for body mass, height, steps and active energy register synchronously in `didFinishLaunchingWithOptions`, including HealthKit background launches. Weight additions and explicit deletions use a paged anchored query. UUID records and the cursor are persisted atomically with the diary; empty/denied reads do not purge weight history. Manual readings remain authoritative for their day. Concurrent events are queued and their completion handlers called once after processing. Cumulative HealthKit statistics avoid summing overlapping phone/watch samples manually. Cached activity is shown in the diary. Activity calories remain informational and are not added twice to a weight plan.

The source uses SwiftUI `scenePhase`, including its initial value, to refresh fully on foreground activation and stop the 30-second activity safety refresh when backgrounded. It does not rely on legacy UIApplicationDelegate foreground callbacks in a scene-based app. The lifecycle correction was reviewed against Apple's SwiftUI lifecycle documentation and compiled successfully against the device SDK, including the current build 24.

Background delivery requests `.immediate` and reports actual registration acceptance, partial acceptance or unavailability. iOS still caps background step-count updates at hourly and decides when delivery occurs. The diary retains complete file protection; locked reads/writes defer to unlock without overwriting existing data. Disconnect removes observers, stops polling and disables background delivery, while retaining history. None of these background behaviors has been tested on a physical iPhone in this environment.

## Health signing path

An installed app needs HealthKit and HealthKit Background Delivery in its final Apple provisioning profile and signature. A locally requested entitlement alone does not grant them. The original HTML of [Apple's supported-capabilities table](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/reference/supported-capabilities-ios/) marks HealthKit as available for free Apple Developer accounts; a paid membership is not established as the cause of the previous missing-entitlement error. An AltStore path that removes Health permissions will still fail regardless of user permission settings.

Impactor 2.6.5 was reviewed at its release tag. Its signer reads binary entitlements, requests matching capabilities through Apple's developer service, obtains an Apple provisioning profile and signs with the profile. The free-account blocked-capability list does not include HealthKit. Its optional Identifier field can preserve the exact installed bundle ID; otherwise it appends the team ID. This is a source-reviewed proposed free Windows path, not proof that Apple approved the user's final profile. See [HEALTH-SETUP-AR.md](HEALTH-SETUP-AR.md) for backup, identifier preservation, installation and device checks.

The app detects a known missing Health entitlement before authorization. Apple intentionally conceals read denial, so completing the permission request is not evidence that readings were granted. Local export.xml import remains a reviewed snapshot fallback and is not presented as automatic synchronization.

## iPhone 16 Pro Max vision memory fix — source and local checks

The reported symptom is termination at model preparation after a completed 3.1 GB download. The prior service admitted based on total physical RAM. That is insufficient: Apple's `os_proc_available_memory()` reports an app's current allowance, which can be lower and can change. Memory pressure is a plausible cause, not a confirmed device diagnosis without its Jetsam/crash report.

The service now selects the compact Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct 4-bit model on 8 GB phones, including iPhone 16 Pro Max, regardless of a transiently larger allowance. It selects 4B on larger devices only when a conservative reserve fits. Both manifests are pinned. The compact model revision is `9c4f5209e57b31f4b9dfba735de3fb983739c9cc`, 1,793,572,102 download bytes, 1,782,040,921 weight bytes; weights' LFS SHA-256 is `4750d95a2162829e127a94e83ac350d498d02070aab216c4687da48804a06ffb`. Initial source verification checked small files and recorded the weight digest from repository LFS metadata. Build 24 subsequently loaded and ran the compact model through the pinned-download path, as confirmed by diagnostics. Already downloaded 4B files are retained.

Admission samples current app allowance before downloads and immediately before loading, with compact fallback if the allowance shrinks. Engineering reserves are 1024 MiB above compact weights and 1792 MiB above full weights. They are conservative thresholds, not measured iPhone peak-memory guarantees. Compact inference uses 560-pixel image processing, 64-token prefill batches and bounded output. The MLX cache is 0 during load and 4 MiB during inference; temporary buffers are cleared between stages and after the model/session scope returns. A 256 MiB low-headroom guard is checked before each inference stage and on streamed output; iOS warnings and leaving the foreground cancel the analysis. Individual synchronous allocations can still precede a warning or check. Requested Increased Memory Limit is optional: free re-signing may omit it, and selection does not infer the allowance from entitlements.

Preview decoding is cached at 640 pixels instead of decoding a 2048-pixel preview on every progress update. An unfinished local run marker permits a conservative retry after termination/force-quit/power loss; it stores only model tier, stage and allowance, no photo or meal description. It is not a crash diagnosis. The optional description remains empty. No user photo is uploaded.
